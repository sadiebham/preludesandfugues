Hel-beast’s Daughter

Prelude

The air stunk of disease and death.

There was a fetid body of water; murky water, that seemed half alive, that moved in odd patterns, as if small things swam against the current beneath the surface.

The world was grey.

There was no natural light, only a heavy greyness that came from above: a fog enshrouded forest, perhaps, though there was no scent of trees.

The girl lifted her head slowly, her antlers weighing heavy.
She was on a stony outcrop overlooking a deep pool.

‘Where am I?’ the girl whispered. ‘Could this be home?’

Fugue

Hode tilted her head this way and that: raised her gaze further.

It might have been a cave she was in, but it was high vaulted and something blocked the entrance.

It smelled like a wet beast, like a diseased stag, a dying dog. Colour gleamed, impossibly high, and slabs of whiteness moved in the dark.

It rumbled.

Hode stared, sinking back onto her haunches.

It moved, and there was an enormous splash, and she automatically moved back, falling backwards, sliding towards the water.

She grabbed at the stone and missed, her hand plunging into the water.

Hode screamed, spreading herself out, shuddering as the tips of her horns breached the surface of the river. She could feel something tugging at them, tugging at her – she pulled back desperately, aware of things rising from the water.

The pain hit her head first, then her shoulders. Whatever had her held her fast. By straining backwards, by pulling with every moment of strength she had, she could hold it from pulling her forwards, from pulling her head-first into the fetid river.

There was a low rumbling from – whatever the looming menace was.

It was – there – but it wouldn’t matter if she couldn’t pull herself free from whatever lurked in the river.

‘I can do this,’ she muttered, managing to get onto her hands and knees.
The stone was cold against her knees.

Unforgiving.

It held her – head lowered, unable to lift her gaze. The pain in her head was increasing. Whatever held her by the horns held enough of them tightly – but there is nothing to grasp, she thought. They are slippery, wet, they should –

She lifted an arm, trying to feel whatever it was that gripped them.

Something wet, furry and bitingly strong wrapped around her wrist and pulled – Hode crashed, belly-first, onto the stone, smacking her chin in the process.

She screamed, fearing the water – and felt herself being lifted.

She blinked the sweat from her eyes as she was lifted up, nearer to – whatever else was in the cave.

Warm, stinking, wet breath gusted across her face.

Hode flinched.

She was lifted – higher.

The impossible light gleamed red, hints of white surrounding it – two of them, massive, each as big as her head – she gulped.

‘Eyes,’ she whispered.

The creature was black fur and talons and red eyes and some kind of tail with which it had grabbed her – and immense slabs of white which were its teeth.

The teeth loomed like some sort of gateway – she trembled in fear. They could cut through her and there was nothing she could do.

She was lifted higher and felt an immense pressure, felt it sniffing, almost threating to tear her from its grasp and into its nostrils.

‘A Hel-beast, a Hel-beast,’ she screamed.

There was a rumbling and a shaking: she was flung from side to side, subject to a wet torrent from its gaping maw and nostrils, and then dropped, unceremoniously, on her back, crying out in shock.

For a moment all was stars and pain.

How did I get here she wondered, before her vision returned.

The beast loomed over her. Its jaw was at least half as long as she was tall, and a thick, slobbering, tongue lolled from it.

A paw, twice her size, pinned her to the stony bank.

Mama, she gulped. You did the best you could. She could feel the paw crushing her.

Hode swatted at it, barely able to make an impression.

‘A Hel-beast. I’m the daughter of a Hel-beast,’ she screamed.

The cave started trembling.

The pressure on her chest eased, but still she was pinned to the ground.

Wet breath slobbered through the air, drenching her face, making her cry out.

The paw was removed – Hode held her breath as the stink intensified, as the immense head lowered.

It was like staring up into a pair of pitch-black cave entrances. She could barely see it, the only light the red of its eyes, but she could smell it, looming over her – a single misplaced paw – it could – she shuddered at the thought.

There are far worse things than Cernunnos in this Forest, she thought.

Something immense swung down – and she found herself being lifted by her head.
Hode screamed, feeling her head being crushed.

She was lifted high, higher than before, the light blooming and brightening – she found herself staring into the creature’s eye, reflecting back at herself, tiny in comparison.

‘God-child,’ it seemed to rumble, or maybe the cave rumbled it, or maybe she imagined it –

And then the pain stopped and she was plummeting – Hode landed flat on her back on something stinky, wet and really foul-smelling; on something unsteady, heaving, shifting –

She flung her hands out for balance and screamed, feeling the thick, wet, stickiness shift underneath her –

There were high points of just-visible white far above her. A yawning emptiness that the thing she was on led into –

Hode screamed again, getting to her feet, falling as quickly, head-first, her horns plunging into the thick, slobbery wetness –

There was a sharp, rumbling, howl of dislike – and then she was flying, part of a wet torrent from the creature’s mouth, drenched in its slobber.

Where can I – she hit something hard and passed out.




Key: Wondering. Fear.
